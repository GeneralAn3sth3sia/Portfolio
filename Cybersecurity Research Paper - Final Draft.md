# **Cybersecurity: Shifting Threat Vectors and Rising Trends** {#cybersecurity:-shifting-threat-vectors-and-rising-trends}

## **Introduction** {#introduction}

As we enter this new age of technologies which seemingly belong in the realm of science fiction, the way we as humans interact with our world is on the verge of changing in ways that are challenging to foresee. Due to the nature of its most apparent applications, AI technologies are having their first breakout effects within the Information Technology fields. This trend is made especially clear in cybersecurity. According to IBM engineer Jeff Crume speaking on the “IBM Technologies” YouTube channel (2026), their researchers estimate that the cost of a data breach, carried out with the involvement of AI, was nearly \$1M greater than those without AI involvement. According to the U.S. Office of the Director of National Intelligence’s (2026) “Annual Threat Assessment of The U.S. Intelligence Community”, “AI is a defining technology of the 21st century…” and with the near constant stream of media coverage of this technology from all angles, it is hard to deny this reality. As we look towards the future, there is another standout technology which is creating waves in the cybersecurity industry: quantum computing. Q-Day, as it has been labeled, is the rapidly approaching day that will see quantum computing break all standard encryption. The emergence of these technologies is highlighting the need for cybersecurity professionals to constantly stay up-to-date with the ever-changing nature of the field. As AI takes over automation of lower level tasks, the field is shifting away from an environment of many employees doing routine analysis and monitoring work, towards a field where cybersecurity professionals are overseeing automatic defenses and applying proactive security solutions to get ahead of threat actors. 

## **Automation in Cyber** {#automation-in-cyber}

According to the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency \[CISA\] (n.d.), one of the core tasks of a cybersecurity defense analyst is to “Provide timely detection, identification, and alerting of possible attacks/intrusions, anomalous activities, and misuse activities and distinguish these incidents and events from benign activities.” These core job duties are not going anywhere, but AI companies have positioned their technologies to change the day-to-day tasks that are used to accomplish this. Artificial Intelligence excels at analyzing and learning from large data sets. It can react nearly instantly to any detected vulnerabilities, exploits, and is even capable of facilitating attack recovery. It will be necessary for corporations and government agencies to take advantage of these strengths in the future, as nothing is stopping hostile entities from using these AI models to near instantly locate and exploit vulnerabilities en masse. Crume, with IBM Technologies, professes that threat actors will soon be able to “...automate the entire killchain” (IBM Technologies, 2025). This will drastically lower the barrier-to-entry for threat actors, increasing the number of so-called “script kiddies” \-threat actors with little to no knowledge of code and who rely on pre-made scripts- while also increasing their ability to successfully carry out an attack.

## **Emergent Attack Surfaces** {#emergent-attack-surfaces}

With AI implementation into security systems comes new vulnerabilities which can be exploited. One of these vulnerabilities comes in the form of prompt injection. This is defined by the Open Worldwide Application Security Project (OWASP) (2026) as “...when input to a large language model (LLM) \[…\] alters the model's behavior in ways the application developer did not intend.” This happens due to an inherent flaw in the way that large language models are capable of processing information: they do not possess the ability to determine the difference between a prompt and data. Imagine you use an AI agent to summarize and sort your emails, and you receive a malicious email. The contents of this email could be formatted in a way that tricks the LLM to read it as a prompt and take action without your approval or knowledge. The importance of proper permissions infrastructure within systems and networks, especially for non-human agents, cannot be understated. If the model has access to sensitive information, it may be able to provide it to whomever injected that prompt.

## **Quantum Resistant Technologies** {#quantum-resistant-technologies}

	In the midst of these new threats posed by AI, there is another problem on the horizon which cybersecurity professionals are already preparing for: cryptographically relevant quantum computers. The transfer to quantum resistant cryptography has already begun within the National Security Systems (NSS). Cryptographically relevant quantum computers, defined by the NSA as a computer “...capable of actually attacking real world cryptographic systems that would be infeasible to attack with a normal computer” (NSA, 2021), are still likely far in the future, but this does not stop malicious parties from harvesting sensitive data now, to be decrypted in the future. The US government is leading the cybersecurity field in this endeavor to replace classical, quantum vulnerable cryptographic solutions with new, quantum resistant forms, with the Commercial National Security Algorithm Suite (CNSA) 2.0 (NSA, 2022). While this suite was created for the use of the NSS, the CNSA has historically set a precedent for the private sector to follow.

## **Conclusion** {#conclusion}

These new threats posed by large language models, alongside the potential automation benefits they promise, highlight the ever-changing nature of the cybersecurity industry and the need to stay educated on the current threat landscape in order to be successful. As automation increases, the rate at which attacks and defensive reactions can take place has increased exponentially. The new vulnerabilities created by integrating AI into systems all over the world are making proper permissions and preemptive network security setups even more vital. With Q-day on the horizon, the industry is set to be completely upended once again, and not for the last time.
